# practice

Fourteen ways one practice is drawn, eight as a calm studio and six at the counter. They differ by what the patient is anxious about and how they arrive: whether they came to choose a person or to get a slot, whether a doctor sent them with a prescription or they are deciding alone and reading for a week first, whether the practice is one therapist's room, a neighbourhood cabinet of five or a walk-in group, in town or the only one in its valley, for adults after work or for children brought by a parent. The four pages and the five prefabs are the same in all fourteen; what changes is whether the first screen leads with a face, a sentence, the hours or the form.

## Cabinet de quartier

The neighbourhood cabinet of four or five therapists that most people mean by a practice: found by a recommendation, checked once before the first call. The first screen is one photograph of the treatment room with the name low on it and today's hours and the door as plain lines along its foot, so nobody scrolls to learn when and where. The team is the page to read, set as a magazine piece with the portraits under the story, because the visitor is choosing a person; the first visit is the long page of questions, and the appointment is one screen again, the form on the page with the hours and the floor beside it. Black, white and one red, square corners: nothing on it sells.

## Heure de parole

A psychotherapist's or a counsellor's practice, where the visitor reads for a week before daring to write and no photograph of a session can be owed. So the opening is words alone, set large, under the name centred like a letterhead; the home page is the long one and both the team and the first visit are read as a journal, the questions already open in the column so that nothing has to be clicked to learn what the first hour costs and whether it is confidential. Aubergine on blush with a little gold, quiet and a little formal; the appointment page sets the invitation beside the form, and the map loses its panel so that the address reads as a line of the letter.

## Mains d’ostéopathe

An osteopathy and manual therapy room sold on craft rather than on a prescription: patients pay themselves, come on a friend's word and want to see the room and the hands before booking. The name is centred over a wide photograph of the room on a single screen, the hours and the door at its foot; teal water on a pale aqua page with airy rounded type, a palette that is good here and right for a room meant to slow people down. The therapists are rows, the portrait beside what each one treats, because there are few of them and each has something to say; the first visit is told as a story, since what happens on the table is the question, and the footer is three short lines.

## Autour de la naissance

Midwives and a pelvic-floor physiotherapist: the practice a woman looks up in the fifth month and studies at length, with a long list of questions and a partner reading over her shoulder. Soft pink-violet with large radii and no borders, friendly rather than clinical. The long home page puts the name beside a portrait, then the week laid across as a strip, since the days decide everything when there is a baby, and a panorama of the streets for whoever comes with a pram; the team is a journal, because she is choosing who will be in the room, the questions run in two columns on their long page, and the appointment sets the invitation beside the form.

## Fauteuil du dentiste

A dental practice of two dentists and a hygienist, where the worry is the chair and the answer is a clean, bright room: cool blue-grey with hairlines, a palette that is only fair for a studio and right for a surgery, and the light is pinned because a dentist's page in the dark reads as a practice that is closed. The long home page sets the name beside the room; the pages sit left of the name and the action right of it, as on the site of a clinic. The team is a plain grid of portraits and the questions, which are many (the insurance, the X-rays, the estimate), run in two columns; the appointment is one screen, the form with the hours and the door beside it, and the footer is three lines.

## Séances du soir

The practice that treats people after work, open until half past eight and looked up from a sofa at ten at night, which is why the dark is pinned: violet and magenta on a night sky, panels edged by a faint glow, a palette that is fair for a studio and right for this hour. One screen, the lit room behind the words with the evening's hours and the door as plain lines at its foot; the newcomer of its street, so the team and the first visit are long pages that say plainly who treats what and what it costs, and the appointment is one screen again. The pages sit left of the name and the action right of it, where a thumb finds it.

## Cabinet de famille

The old house: a practice a mother opened thirty years ago and a daughter runs now, in a village house with its name on a brass plate, where half the patients were treated as children. Terracotta and olive on warm paper with a chiselled serif, hand-made and unhurried. The first screen is the house's photograph with the name centred over it like a sign, the hours and the door under it; the team and the first visit are both told as stories, the therapists as rows in the narrow column, because this practice's argument is that it has known its patients for a long time. Only the appointment is a long page, the invitation beside the form, for those who would still rather telephone.

## Sur ordonnance

The plain physiotherapy practice most patients are sent to by their doctor: they hold a prescription for nine sessions and want three things, whether it is open, whether insurance pays and the telephone number. So the name stands beside the room's photograph on one half of the first screen and the hours and the door on the other, as plain lines; the first visit repeats that opening with every answer already open under it, and the appointment puts the bare form beside the hours. Warm paper with a burnt-sienna accent and a serif, the counter's best palette; the therapists are rows saying what each treats, and the footer is three lines because nobody reads further.

## Physio du sport

A sports physiotherapy practice beside the stadium, for runners and footballers who want to know who treats a tendon and how early in the morning they can come. Sunny yellow on cream with black keycap buttons and thick ink edges, loud and hands-on. The long home page opens on a photograph across the whole width with the words over it, and the week runs straight under it as the counter's strip, the 7:30 starts in plain sight; the team and the questions are long pages, the questions in two columns, and the appointment puts the form beside today's hours and the door. The pages sit left of the name and the action right of it.

## Cabinet du village

The only practice in its valley, open three days a week above the post office: patients drive twenty minutes to it and want the days, the road and where to park, in that order. Deep green on warm paper with drawn borders and hard shadows, sturdy and plain. The home page is the long one, the week laid across as a strip under the opening and then a panorama of the roads around the door; the first visit holds its questions beside the hours, the appointment is long too, the invitation beside the form and the panorama again, and everything in the footer sits on one centred column, as on the village's own notice board.

## Permanence

A group practice by the station that takes people without an appointment, seven days a week until late, and is looked up on a phone by somebody in pain at eleven at night: the dark is pinned, indigo with the rust of a rivet and stitched panels. No photograph is owed, so the opening is a statement, the words set large on one half and tonight's hours and the door on the other; the questions are many and practical and run in two columns on a long page, and the appointment puts the bare form beside the hours for the few who do book ahead. The pages sit left of the name, the action right of it, the map has no panel and the footer is three lines.

## Petits patients

A practice for children, a speech therapist, a paediatric physiotherapist and an osteopath for infants, chosen by a parent who has to say "it will be fine" to a four-year-old. Peach paper and raspberry in a rounded face with stickers for panels, a palette that is only fair for a counter and right here, because the page has to look like somewhere a child would walk into. The name is centred over a wide photograph of the playroom with the hours and the door beside it, and the name sits centred over the pages too; the first visit is a long page with the few questions every parent asks already open, and the appointment is long as well, the invitation beside the form, with everything in the footer on one centred column.

## Médecine du voyage

A travel clinic of two doctors and a nurse: the vaccines, the malaria tablets and the yellow-fever certificate, booked by people who have just bought a ticket and need the first injection four weeks before they fly. Green and orange on a tinted page, a palette that is good at the counter and right for a page that should feel like the start of the trip rather than a waiting room. The words are centred over a wide photograph on a long home page with the week across it as a strip; the doctors are portraits on the long page, the first visit opens on the hours and the door beside the name with the many questions (which vaccines for which country, what they cost, whether insurance pays) set in two columns under them, and the appointment is the form beside its invitation, the map on the page with no panel round it. The pages sit left of the name and the action right of it.

## Consultation mémoire

A memory clinic, a geriatrician, a neuropsychologist and a nurse, read first not by the patient but by a daughter or a son who has noticed that their father repeats himself and does not know how to bring him. Warm paper with a burnt-sienna accent and a serif display face, a palette that is good for a studio and right for a page that has to be read slowly and kindly. Home is the long page, the words beside a photograph of the consulting room under the name centred like a letterhead; the team is told person by person in a magazine's column, each a portrait beside what they will ask, and the first visit is a letter too, every answer open (what to bring, how long it takes, whether the patient has to agree). The appointment is one quiet screen, the hours as plain lines, the form on the page with no panel round it and a panorama of the streets around the door, since the visit is often the patient's first into town in a while. The footer is one centred column.
