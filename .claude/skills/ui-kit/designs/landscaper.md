# landscaper

Fifteen ways one garden firm is drawn, six on the trail, five at the market and four in the yard. The pages and the prefabs are the same in all of them; what changes is which firm it is (the village paysagiste, the pond builder, the dry-stone mason, the designer, the orchard planter, the nursery, the playground builder, the tree surgeon, the irrigation fitter, the group that holds contracts for property managers) and so what the first screen leads with: a garden in this month's light, the plants, a figure, a sentence or the way to the yard. The gardens are read as a story told through a year, as panels on a board, as photographs beside their facts or as a ruled record, and the visit is either one short screen away or at the end of a long page the visitor has been walked down.

## Jardins de saison

The village paysagiste most briefs describe: a family firm of ten to twenty that draws, plants, paves and comes back to prune, known within twenty kilometres. The cover is one garden in this month's light with the name over it and the season's line above the bar, the yard and the nursery along its foot, and below it the first client's words set large, because a garden firm is chosen on what somebody said two summers later. The gardens page reads like a magazine piece, the story of one garden over a band of the same ground in three seasons and the record under it; the questions and the request are short pages that end at the footer, since this visitor already knows the name from a van in the lane.

## Bassins et baignades

A firm that digs ponds and natural swimming pools: an order of sixty to a hundred thousand francs that a couple studies for a week before writing. The long page suits them: the opening centres its words over one wide photograph of water, then the offer, the clients and the streets round the yard across the page, and the ask waits at the end. The questions are an article with every answer open, since filtration, permits and the first winter are read in full and not clicked through, and the request sets the invitation on one side and the form on the other. Lagoon is only good on the trail, but water is the subject here and its blue-green is the one colour the page needs.

## Pierre sèche

An old house, three generations of garden masons: dry-stone walls, vineyard terraces, steps in Jura stone. Its clients own an old property and fear a wall that bulges, so the first screen sets the name beside the address of the yard, the map plain with no panel round it, and the header carries the name in the middle the way the firm's letterhead does. The work is a few large jobs down a long page, each photograph beside its place, its year and its metres; the questions run in two columns and the form stands bare on the page. Sandstone turns the trail's horizon to the colour of the stone itself.

## Atelier de paysage

A landscape designer's studio: two people who draw gardens and supervise the firms that build them, selling on craft to clients who read every page before they call. There is no cover photograph: the sentence stands alone, as large as a title page, with the address at its foot; the offer is a handful of tiles, and each garden is told as a story with its pictures in one band. The questions are an essay on a garden's calendar before they are a list, and the request closes a long page with the invitation beside the form. Pinned dark on clay, the way a portfolio is shown: the photographs are the only light on the page.

## Jardin vivant

The newcomer, three years old: a gardener who turns lawns into meadows, plants native hedges and uses no pesticide, and whose clients found her over a neighbour's fence. The first screen sets the promise beside one photograph, then the offer, many short voices flowing down a wall and the map across the page; the gardens are told as before-and-after stories, the questions stand all open down a wide page because the same seven are asked at every gate, and the request is one short screen with a compact footer under it. Fresh is only fair on the trail, its green louder than the landscape behind it, and that is why it is here: this firm sells green. Pinned light, a meadow at noon.

## La pépinière

A nursery that plants what it grows: people come to the yard on a Saturday morning to choose a tree, and the garden follows from the tree. The home page is a board of glass panels, what the firm does as tiles under their icons, the clients, the way in, with the season's line above the bar; the gardens are a board too, each a panel sized by its one figure. The questions and the request each sit under a short opening, as at a counter, and the footer centres the name over the address. Orchard on the wash of the palette: leaves and fruit, the market's own colours for a firm with soil under its nails.

## Potagers et vergers

A gardener of kitchen gardens and orchards: raised beds, espaliers, a school's vegetable plot, families who want to eat from their own ground. The first screen sets the name beside the nursery's map, drawn plain, and the header puts the name in the middle like the front of a seed packet; under it, short words from many clients on a wall. Each garden is a row down a long page, its photograph beside what was planted and on how much ground; the questions are all open, since when to plant is the whole trade, and the form stands bare on the page before the season's band. Fresh, the market's best, for a firm whose proof ends in a salad bowl.

## Élagage et abattage

A tree surgeon: two climbers, a platform, a chipper and a phone that rings after every storm. The cover is a climber in the crown under the hazard strip, the name as large as on the truck's door, the yard plain at its foot. The record is a ruled index with no photographs, tree by tree, by place, year and metres, the way a commune's inventory reads; the questions stand open because everybody asks the same one, whether a permit is needed, and the answer has to be read before a saw is booked; the form is bare and the footer compact. Marigold is the yard's hazard colour and this trade's own.

## Régies et copropriétés

A group of forty gardeners holding upkeep contracts for property managers, co-ownerships and communes: it sells on a fixed yearly price and the same team every fortnight. No photograph opens the page: the promise is set alone over a board where the offer is rows with their prices, the administrators speak on a wall and the streets round the depot run wide. The record is an index by place and surface under a short opening, the questions fill two columns, and the header is set as a group's, the pages at the left. Denim, the blue of the overalls, takes the hazard out of the yard and leaves the paperwork.

## Vergers hautes-tiges

A firm that plants and prunes standard fruit trees: old orchards brought back by farmers whom the canton pays for them, communes replanting a meadow, owners of one pear tree older than their house. The first screen sets the name beside an orchard in blossom on one side and the yard and the pruning season on the other, and one farmer's sentence carries the home page under it. Deep green on warm paper with borders that look drawn, a palette that is good on the trail and right for a firm that still writes its labels by hand; each orchard planted is set across the page with its photograph beside its facts (the commune, the number of trees, the varieties grafted), the questions (the subsidy, winter pruning, the grafts) are told in one column as a letter would answer them, and the visit is asked for at the end of the long page with no panel round the form, the yard on the map with none round it either.

## Cours d’école

A firm that builds natural playgrounds and school gardens: willow huts, sand and water, a vegetable bed for each class, ordered by communes and kindergartens and argued over by parents' associations. Peach paper, raspberry and a cool blue in a rounded face with stickers for panels, a palette that is good at the market and right for a page the children will be shown too. Home is a board seen at once under the words centred over a wide photograph of a playground at break time: what the firm does as four large icons, the teachers' and parents' short words on a wall, the yard as a panorama. The playgrounds made are another board, the many questions a commune asks before its budget vote (the norms, falls, upkeep, the cost) are set in two columns on the long page, and the figure is asked for beside its invitation; the name is centred over the pages like the sign over a school gate.

## Arrosage et drainage

The technical firm of the trade: automatic watering, drainage, robot mowers and the pumps of garden ponds, installed and then serviced under contract for facility managers and for owners whose lawn died in a dry July. No photograph opens the page: the promise is set alone, large, in a mono face on near-black with one blue, pinned dark like the controller in the garage, a palette that is good in the yard and right for a firm that sells the system rather than the plants. What the firm does is ruled as a list read down, the installations are a ruled record with the photographs dropped, each with its zones and its litres, and the questions about winterising and the guarantee are set in two columns under an opening that gives the service line and the yard. The visit is asked for with no panel round the form, the map is on the page with none round it, the pages sit left of the name and the action right of it, and the footer is three lines.
