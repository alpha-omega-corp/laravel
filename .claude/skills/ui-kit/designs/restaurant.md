# restaurant

Fourteen ways one restaurant is drawn, six by lamplight, four at the counter and four in the sun. They differ by what the visitor came to check and when: whether the place is a room booked for the evening, a counter looked up at a quarter to twelve or a kitchen that cooks what the market had; whether the house is old or new, in town or an hour's drive away, sold on price or on craft; and whether the page is read on a phone at the door or at a desk a week ahead. The pages and the prefabs are the same in all fourteen; what changes is which fact the first screen leads with.

## Salle du soir

A dinner room worth photographing, in town: the cover is one photograph with the name over it, tonight's hours and the door as plain lines along its foot, and the carte is one quiet column read like a wine list. For a place people book for the evening, where the room is half of what they come for; the house's story is a page to read, and the visit page stands the form on the page beside the hours, so a table is asked for without scrolling.

## Grande table

The old house with a tasting menu, booked three weeks ahead and studied before it is. The opening is words alone, set large, because the name is the photograph, with tonight's hours and the door held beside it; black, white and one red, square corners, the name centred over the pages like a letterhead. The story is read as a journal, and the visit page is the long one, the invitation on one side and the form on the other, since asking for a table is the one thing this site is for.

## Cave à manger

A wine bar that cooks, the newcomer of its street: a few plates and forty bottles, nobody books, and it is found on a phone from the pavement at ten at night. One screen says it all, the name centred over the room with the hours and the door at its foot, and the plates are cards worth looking at rather than a list to study. Aubergine and antique gold; the pages sit left of the name and the action right of it, and the footer is three short lines because every page already closes on the band above it.

## Auberge de campagne

A country inn in the same family for three generations, an hour's drive out and closed half the week: the visitor wants to know that it is worth the road, which days, and which road. So the home page is the long one, the house's photograph first, then the week laid across as a strip and a panorama of the lanes around the door; the carte is long (the game, the Sunday menu) and sets its sections side by side. Terracotta and olive by lamplight, a palette that is good here rather than best and right for a house built of the same earth; the story is read as a journal and the table is asked for beside its invitation.

## Bistrot du coin

The neighbourhood bistro, open at noon and at night, whose regulars only ever check one thing: is it open, and is there a table. The name beside the room's photograph on one side, tonight's hours and the door on the other, on the home page and again on the visit page, where the form stands on the page with no panel round it. Warm paper tones and a serif on the dark; the house is a long page of the suppliers as doors rather than a story, and the footer is kept to three lines.

## Table à la ferme

A farm that cooks what it grows, open from Friday to Sunday at the end of a lane: people drive out for it and want the produce, the days and the way in, in that order. The long home page opens on the farm's photograph behind the words, then the bento of what is in season, then the hours beside the map on one board. Deep green on warm paper; the carte's sections are the glass cards the direction draws, the visit page holds a plain form beside the hours and the lane, and everything in the footer sits on one centred column.

## Comptoir à sushis

Ten seats at a counter in front of the chef, two sittings a night, no menu to choose from, and every seat of the month taken the evening the month opens. Violet on a night sky, a palette that is good by lamplight and right for a room where the only light falls on the board; no photograph is owed, so the opening is one sentence set large with the two sittings and the door at its foot, under the name centred like the sign over the door. The season's courses are cards worth looking at one by one, the chef and the fish market are a story with its pictures in one row, and the visit is two halves, the sittings beside the door, with the request for a seat on the page under them and no panel round it. The footer is three lines.

## Camion-cuisine

A food truck that stands in a different square each day of the week and cooks for weddings and office parties on Saturdays: its regulars want to know where it is today, and everybody else wants to book it. Peach paper, raspberry and a cool blue in a rounded face with stickers for panels, a palette that is good at the market and right for a truck painted the same colours. The first screen sets the name beside the truck at its hatch, with the week across the page as a strip, one cell a day with the square written in it; the dishes are a few cards worth looking at, the truck's story is the long page, and the contact page opens on the week and today's square drawn wide before the invitation to book it for a private day, the form beside it. The pages sit left of the name and the action right of it, and the footer is three lines.
