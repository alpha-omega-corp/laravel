# spa

Fifteen ways one spa is drawn: six as a calm studio, five as an evening room, four as baths outdoors. The pages and the prefabs are the same in all of them; what changes is what the visitor is taken to have come for (the water, a pair of hands, the price of two hours, the view from the bath, a week of cure, an evening for two), and so what the first screen leads with and how the treatments and the entries are read: as a price list, as cards, or as rows with the price at the end.

## Maison de bains

The destination day spa most people mean by the word: a long first page that opens on the pool photographed with air around it, then the three doors (treatments, entries, the place), the week and a wide map of the way in. The treatments are a price list with dotted leaders from the top of their page, the place is a story broken once by its pictures, and every answer about what to bring is left open. For a house people book for a Saturday and read about during the week before.

## Cabinet de massage

One therapist or a handful, two cabins and no pool to photograph: the home page is the practitioner's story in a narrow column, her portrait beside the words, and the treatments are the price list straight away. Terracotta and olive on warm paper, because what is sold is made by hand; the entries and the booking are quiet pages in the same column, the form with no panel round it. For a practice that sells on craft, where the visitor is choosing the hands they will trust their back to.

## Institut de jour

A day spa in town that lives on gift cards and lunch-hour treatments: soft colour, the words centred over a wide photograph, the week laid across the page and each treatment a card worth looking at. The entries are a list from the top of their page with the questions in two columns under it, and the place is one cover and a strip of pictures, because this visitor is often buying an hour for somebody else and wants to see the hour before the room.

## Bains de quartier

Public baths that sell on price to people who come every week: no photograph at all, the name and one sentence set large, with the week and the way in at the foot of the first screen. The entries are rows with the price at the end of each and the treatments two columns of a list, both from the top of their page. For municipal or cooperative baths whose regulars ask whether it is open and what ten entries cost; the neutral blue-grey says a service, not a treat.

## Nocturnes

A newcomer that sells its late sessions: pinned dark, the pool at night bled to the window's edges as one cover, violet on a night sky, with tonight's hours and the door along its foot, because the question is until when. The sessions are cards, the entries a quiet column under a few lines on which evenings are the calm ones, and the place a second cover and a mosaic. For baths or a sauna house with a young crowd that decides on the day, on a phone, with friends; the studio's air keeps it from looking like a nightclub.

## Vieux bains

For a family establishment with a history worth telling, whose visitors read for a while before they come and come back for thirty years.

## Hammam

A hammam is dark and warm before anybody designs a page for it: aubergine and antique gold by lamplight, a little theatrical, the name centred over the pages. The first page is long because a first-timer needs the ritual shown: the heated stone bled across the opening, the three doors, then the week as a strip, since which evening is the women's is the question asked most, and the streets round the door as a panorama. The rituals are one quiet column under their own opening, the entries are told before they are priced, and the rooms are a mosaic. For a hammam or an oriental bath in a town's back street.

## Thalasso

A thalassotherapy institute on the seafront, where people come for six days of seawater, wraps and jets rather than an afternoon: the cure is chosen in January for a week in May, often by somebody's doctor or for somebody's mother. The first screen centres the name over a wide photograph of the pools with the sea behind the glass, and the place is told like a magazine, the narrow column broken by a band of the treatment rooms. Teal water and a coral sun, the sea's own colours, airy and slow. The treatments are a price list with the sections side by side, seawater beside massage beside the doctor's; the cures read as a story with each programme in rows, its price at the end, and the questions on prescriptions in two columns; and the booking is a long page with the invitation beside the form.

## Suite privée

A private spa suite in town, rented by the hour for two: a hot tub, a sauna and a massage table behind one locked door, booked for an anniversary or given as a present, almost always for an evening. Dark by design, violet and magenta edged by a faint glow, the night-out palette rather than the bath-house one. The first screen sets the suite on one side and tonight's free slots and the door on the other; the formulas, two hours, the evening and the night, are rows with the price at the end, and the questions everybody asks before booking (towels, champagne, a gift card) are all open. The suite is one photograph and its name; the treatments are a few cards; the request for a slot stands beside its invitation; and the pages sit left of the name with the action right of it.

## Sources chaudes

Hot springs in the garrigue, pools carved in the rock with a mud bath under the fig trees, open from March to November and reached by a track: the visitor comes for the day from forty kilometres around and wants the season's hours, the price of a day and where to leave the car. The landscape lies behind the name across the opening, the season's week follows as a strip, and the map is a panorama of the track to the gate. Terracotta and olive on warm paper, the colour of the mud itself. The treatments are a short list of cards on a long page; the entries are rows with their price at the end; the place is a long page of pictures with every fifth one large; and the booking is told like a journal, the form under the story of the day.
