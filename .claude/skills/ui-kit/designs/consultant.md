# consultant

Fifteen ways one consultancy is drawn, across the three looks it may take. What they differ by is who is hiring and how that person decides: a committee comparing three offers for a week, a board chair with a vacancy on Monday, an owner of sixty who reads every line, a private person paying for six sessions out of their own pocket. That decides whether the first screen leads with standing, with one promise or with a face; whether the offer is a priced list read down, a few tiles or a board; whether the method is told as a letter or laid out as a road; and how much is said before the form asks for a first conversation.

## Cabinet établi

The small advisory firm chosen on standing, ten years in and hired on a colleague's word. A managing director looks it up to check it is what he was told: the long home page says what problem it solves, then proves it with the bodies it belongs to ruled as rows and one client's words set large, before it asks for anything. The services are a list with a price on every line, the method is told as a letter down one rule, and the request for a first conversation stands beside its invitation. Bone paper and blue-black ink, the most expected drawing of this business and the one to start from.

## Lettre aux dirigeants

An old practice of one or two partners that has never advertised. Its clients are owners in their sixties who read every line and distrust anything that looks sold, so the home page is a letter: the firm told in one measured column before a single proof, under an opening that is words alone. The fees are a list to be read down, the method stands beside its opening with every answer already open, and even the contact page explains the first conversation before it shows a form drawn plain on the page. Monochrome with one red, and pinned light, because a letter is ink on paper.

## Mandats publics

A consultant who lives on tenders: communes, foundations, hospitals, where a committee of five compares three offers on a shared screen for a week and files the page with the rest. The home page opens on who the firm is and goes straight to its references ruled as rows, each with its year and its scope, and several short voices rather than one. The fees are a list, the method is the long page with its five steps across in a row of equal weight and its questions in two columns to be scanned, and the footer is compact because every page closes above it. Cool blue-grey and dense, like the documents it will sit among.

## Intérim de direction

An interim manager: one person, hired for six months to hold a post between two holders. The visitor is a board chair with a vacancy on Monday who wants a face, a date and a telephone number, so the first screen is the name beside the portrait under the availability line, then the houses already served as one muted row of names. The offer is a handful of tiles because it is one job in a few shapes, the road is a short row beside its opening, and the contact page is the long plain one with the form on the page itself, since this visitor has already decided to call. Warm printed paper, so one person does not look like a firm.

## Maisons de famille

The home page tells the firm's story under one photograph that fills the opening before anything is proved, the offer is a few tiles because a family comes for one thing, the method is the long page closing on its questions in two columns, and the first conversation is asked for beside a few warm lines.
