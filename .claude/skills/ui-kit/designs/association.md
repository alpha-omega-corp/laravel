# association

Twelve ways one association is drawn: seven as the notice board people trust, five as the stall in the open. The pages and the prefabs are the same in all twelve; what changes is what the visitor came for (a date, a door, a name to write to, a way to help, a way to give), so which page gets the list, which gets the board and which fact the first screen leads with, and how loud the whole thing is allowed to be.

## Maison de quartier

The neighbourhood association as most towns know it, friendly rather than official: green and orange on a tinted page, and the direction left to draw everything its own way. Home is a board of notices under the framed opening (what is on, how to help, who runs it, the door on the map), the agenda is the list its page is for, and the committee and the giving page each get a full opening before their one list. For an association that has had a hall and a weekly desk for decades, whose visitor is a neighbour checking a date.

## Fondation

A foundation that lives on a few donors and answers to them, and the one design here meant to look official: cool blue-grey and hairline rules, the first screen one sentence saying what it is for with no photograph to soften it, the council a list of people with a line each, and the giving page setting the words beside the account at the page's full width. For a foundation or a charity whose reader studies it for a week before giving, and whose few dates each deserve a card.

## Paroisse

A parish or a community of faith: calm water colours, the name centred like a letterhead, and on the first screen what it is beside where it meets. The agenda is the season read down a rule, and the people who can be written to are a list, a name and what to ask each of them. For a parish whose visitor is new in town and wants the door, the hour and somebody to write to, in that order.

For a club whose members look up Saturday's outing on a phone, and whose open roles (a leader, a hut warden) each need a paragraph.

## Société de village

The old society everybody in the village already knows: deep green on warm paper with drawn borders, everything centred as on its letterhead, the fête photographed across the window, and under it the long page with a panorama of the streets round the hall. The dates stay the direction's rows on their date tiles, the pictures are a strip between the committee and the story. For a development society, a youth society or a brass band a century old, read top to bottom once a year.

## Jeune collectif

A newcomer: founded two years ago, no history to tell, no archive of photographs, a lot of calls for hands. Peach paper, a raspberry link and stickers for panels, playful and clear as a Saturday; the opening is one sentence set large, home and the agenda are each a board, and the page that matters is the one to take part: its opening, then each role a card. For a parents' group, a climate collective or a street that just got organised, whose visitor arrives from the code on a poster.

## Atelier ouvert

A place where people make and mend together: indigo on a washed page with the rust of a rivet, panels edged with two lines of stitching, as sturdy and plain-spoken as the bench. The first screen says what the workshop is beside where it is, the open days are the list their page is for, and the people who hold the tools are a list with what each can teach. For a repair café, a bike workshop or a toy library, a site made to be used rather than admired.

## Ciné-club

A society that happens after dark: pinned dark, violet and magenta on a night sky, the name set large and alone as on a typeset poster, and on the first screen the hall beside it, the map bare with the address under it. Every evening is a card, the pictures a strip, the roles (the booth, the till, the bar) a card each. For a film club, a concert society or a theatre troupe, found on a phone on the way to the door; the palette is only fair on this direction and earns its place at night.
