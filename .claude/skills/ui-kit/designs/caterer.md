# caterer

Twelve ways one caterer is drawn, across the three looks it may take: by lamplight for the house that sells an evening, in editorial calm for the kitchen that sells its plates, and in the open for the one that cooks the season. The pages and the prefabs are the same in all twelve; what changes is who is asking (a couple with a year ahead, an office with a date on Friday, a family with eighty guests and no hall) and so what each page leads with: a photograph, a price a head, somebody else's word, or the form.

## Table du soir

The established traiteur whose trade is the seated dinner and the wedding: the cover is one photograph of a table laid by candlelight with the name and the ask on it, and nothing else on the first screen. The formulas are read down hearth's one quiet column like a wine list, the receptions are told like a magazine, and the questions and the form each stand straight under their opening, the invitation beside the form. For a house people choose with their eyes first and a calculator second, so the strongest host's word is set large right under the cover.

## Maison de famille

A family firm in its third generation, passed from village hall to village hall by word of mouth: the home opens with the words beside the photograph and goes straight to what hosts said, since that is how this house has always been chosen. The formulas stand side by side like the price sheet handed over a counter, every answer is printed open for hosts who read rather than click, and the request reads as a letter: what happens next, then the form. Sandstone keeps hearth's lamp but warms it to the colour of a dining room at home rather than a restaurant.

## Chef à domicile

A private chef who cooks in the host's own kitchen for eight to twenty: one person, chosen on craft and on trust. The first screen is a sentence set large with no production photograph behind it, the long home explains how an evening goes, and the formulas are cards worth reading one by one. The hosts' short words are a wall, the pictures a strip that pauses the story, and the form stands plain on the page, as a note written to one person would.

## Fête du personnel

The events kitchen that lives on company dinners and year-end parties: whoever organises has a date, a headcount and a budget line, and wants a firm figure by Friday. The home leads with the name centred over the hall and then with what other companies said; the questions are a long list scanned in two columns, and the form stands straight under its opening. Nocturne is good rather than best here and is the right one: a night blue that reads as a December evening in a rented hall, cooler than the ember of a restaurant.

## Noces au château

A house that only does weddings, for couples who have a year ahead and come back ten times: the home is the long page they scroll with their parents, each wedding is told as a story, and the answers come after the account of how the months of preparation go. The request is written like a letter, once they have read what happens next. Blossom is only fair under hearth and is chosen on purpose: a dusty rose on the dark is flowers by candlelight, and says wedding before a word is read.

## Vernissage

A newcomer in town who does one thing, the standing cocktail, for openings, launches and agencies: the poster says it on one screen, the words beside the photograph. The pieces are cards, the evenings a mosaic, and the form sits beside its invitation with the footer cut short under it. Pinned dark, with lagoon: these are evening jobs photographed under spotlights, and the teal on black reads as a bar rather than a kitchen.

## Grande brigade

A group with several kitchens that serves congresses and company events up to four hundred covers, read by a buyer who compares three suppliers. The home is the long page with the name centred, the formulas open their page side by side to be compared line for line, the questions are a long list in two columns, and the request explains the procedure before it asks. Harbour is only fair under studio, since navy cools the plates; it is kept because that buyer expects a supplier's blue and not a boutique's cream.

## Midi au bureau

The kitchen that sells on price: buffets and standing lunches delivered to offices, ordered by an assistant between two meetings. The first screen is a sentence set large and the offices' own word straight under it; the formulas stand side by side to be compared, the few questions everybody asks are answered in full, and the form is plain and first on its page. Pinned light: it is read at a desk at noon, and fresh is the green of a salad in daylight.

Pinned dark, with marigold: the page is the square at dusk and the highlight is the flame.
