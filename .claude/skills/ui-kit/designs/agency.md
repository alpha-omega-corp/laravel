# agency

Twelve ways one agency is drawn, across the three looks it may take: a product's front page for the agency that sells a result, white walls for the one whose work is looked at, and a calm magazine for the one that is read. The pages and the prefabs are the same in all of them; what changes is what the first screen leads with — a promise, one piece of work, a sentence or a price — and how the work and the services are read: on a board, down a long page, as a ruled index or as a price list.

## Agence web

The web agency of a dozen people that most businesses end up calling: one promise set large, the clients' marks straight under it, one client's sentence across the column, and the brief as the same button from the first screen to the last. For an agency that sells sites and shops to firms comparing three offers in an evening: the work is a board to scan, the services a long page with the road just above the ask, and the brief page is its opening and the form, nothing to scroll past.

## Jeune pousse

Three people, a year old, selling a fixed price and a short deadline: the promise centred over one wide picture, the offer as tiles with a price on each, the steps joined in one row, and the founders as rows with a line each because there are only three of them. For the newcomer whose clients are shops, associations and other young firms, who ask what it costs and who will answer; the green and orange are louder than this look likes, which is the point — it should not pass for a bank's agency.
