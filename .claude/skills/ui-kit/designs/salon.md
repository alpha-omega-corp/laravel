# salon

Fifteen ways one salon is drawn, eight as a studio and seven at the counter. They differ by what is sold and to whom: a cut, a colour, a razor, a wedding morning, a coat of polish or a cut at home; whether the visitor chooses a salon by its photographs over a week or looks one up from the pavement to know whether a chair is free; whether the house is old or new, in town, in a village or on the seafront, sold on craft or on price. The pages and the prefabs are the same in all fifteen; what changes is which of a salon's three questions (what does the work look like, what does it cost, when is somebody in) the first screen answers.

## Salon de ville

The salon most people mean by the word: cuts and colour for a town's regulars, five chairs and an appointment book. The first screen is one photograph of the room with the name low on it and, along its foot, the week's hours and the door, so whoever only came to check those never scrolls. The prices are a list with dotted leaders from the top of their page; the work is told like a magazine, every fifth picture twice the size; the people are a long page of portraits; and the appointment is asked for on a long page too, the invitation on one side and the form on the other. Black, white and one red, so the hair is the only colour on the page.

## Coloriste

A colourist whose balayage takes an afternoon and is chosen after a week of comparing photographs on a sofa: the salon that sells on craft. Nobody arrives here by chance, so nothing is squeezed onto one screen. The home page is the long one, the name centred over a wide photograph of the work, then the three doors, the week laid across as a strip and a panorama of the streets around the door. The prices are a long page with the sections side by side, because a colour is priced against the cut and the care that go with it; the work and the people are two stories, each broken once by a band of pictures, and the pictures are all one size so a blond is compared with a blond. Aubergine with a little antique gold. Only the appointment is short: one screen, and the form under it.

## Barbier

A barber's shop of dark wood and leather: a cut, a beard, a straight razor and a hot towel, for men who come every three weeks and book from a phone on the way to work. The dark is pinned because the shop is dark, and oxblood and gold are the chair's own colours. The first screen sets the name beside one photograph of that chair, with the hours and the door as plain lines along its foot; the prices are one narrow list from the top of their page. The barbers are rows, a portrait beside what each does best, since the visitor picks one of three; the work is a single row of pictures that scrolls, a pause rather than a portfolio; and the appointment is one screen with a bare form. The pages sit left of the name and the action right of it, and the footer is three lines.

## Jour de noces

A salon that lives on wedding mornings: the chignon, a trial six weeks before, the mother and two witnesses in the same booking. It is found in spring and studied until autumn, so the home page is the long one, the photograph behind the words and the name centred over the pages like the head of an invitation, closing on a panorama of the streets because on the day somebody has to park. Soft pink with no borders at all, a palette that is good here rather than best and right for a page that has to feel like that morning. The price list keeps its narrow column so the trial's price is found at once; the work and the people read as two magazine stories, every fifth picture large; and the appointment page is long, the invitation beside the form, since a date is asked for a year ahead and never in one line.

## Onglerie

A nail studio opened last year by two people in their twenties, found on a phone between a photograph of somebody's hands and the question of whether there is a slot tonight. Violet and magenta, a palette that is only fair for a studio and right for this one: the work is colour, and the page should look like the shelf of bottles. One screen says it, the name centred over a wide photograph of hands with the hours and the door at its foot. The list is short, so each service is a card rather than a line, on a long page; the work is told like a magazine with every fifth set of nails twice the size; and the appointment is one more screen with a bare form. The pages sit left of the name and the action right of it, under a thumb.

## Coiffure végétale

A salon that colours with plants and clay, cuts dry and sells no sulphates: its visitors read before they book, because a henna takes three hours and cannot be undone the week after. So the home page is the long one, the name beside a photograph of the powders, then what the salon does differently, then the week laid across as a strip. Terracotta and olive on warm paper, good here rather than best, and the colours of what is in the bowls. The price list is one narrow column; the people are a story, each a row with a portrait beside what she does, because who mixes the paste matters more than the room; the work is a long page with its pictures in one row that scrolls; and the appointment is a single screen.

## Maître coiffeur

The old house: a master hairdresser's salon in the same family since the grandfather, whose clients have had the same chair for thirty years and whose name on the window is the whole advertisement. So the opening is that name in words alone, set large, under a header that centres it over the pages like a letterhead, with the hours and the door as plain lines along the foot of the screen. Warm paper, burnt sienna and a serif, as if printed. The prices are a long page with the sections side by side, ladies beside gentlemen; the people are the story of the house, read as a journal; the work and the appointment are long quiet pages, the form on the page with no panel round it.

## Salon du quartier

The neighbourhood salon, the plain counter: the same forty streets of clients, who check three things and in this order: is it open, what does a cut cost, can I come on Saturday. The name stands beside a photograph of the window with the facts under it, and the week's hours follow at once as the counter's own strip; the prices are rows from the top of their page; and the long page that asks for the appointment sets the invitation beside the form. Warm paper with a burnt-sienna accent, framed and practical; the map is drawn bare with the address under it, because everybody already knows the corner.

## Sans rendez-vous

The salon that sells on price and takes whoever walks in: a cut at a fixed price, no appointment needed, looked up from the pavement to know whether it is open and how long the wait is. No photograph is owed, so the opening is a statement, the words set large, with the week's hours as a strip under it and a panorama of the streets around the door for whoever is coming on foot. Sunny yellow with thick ink edges, like a price painted on the window. The prices are rows from the top of their page; the work is one row of pictures that scrolls, since nobody chooses this salon by its portfolio; the form is bare, for the few who do book; and the footer is three lines.

## Coiffeur des familles

Wednesday afternoon and Saturday morning: a salon where a first haircut is an event, with a booster seat, a certificate and a parent's cut in the next chair. Peach paper and raspberry in a rounded face, a palette that is only fair for a counter and right for this one, and the light is pinned because a page that has to say "bring them" does not go dark. The name stands beside a photograph of a child in the big chair, under a header that centres the name over the pages like a shop sign; the prices sit side by side by who they are for; the work is a wall of pictures with every fifth one large, because parents look for a child like theirs; and the appointment page sets the invitation beside the form, since a family books three chairs at once. Everything in the footer sits on one centred column.

## Coupe du soir

A salon that opens at noon and closes at nine, for people who cannot leave work: it is looked up at seven in the evening on a phone in the tram, which is why the dark is pinned. Indigo with the rust of a rivet, sturdy and plain-spoken. The name is centred over a wide photograph of the lit window, with the hours as plain lines, because the only question is until when; the list is short and every service a card with its price; the people are rows, a portrait beside what each does, since the visitor books whoever is in that evening. The pages sit left of the name and the action right of it, and the footer is three lines.

## Salon du village

A village salon with two chairs, open three and a half days a week, whose hairdresser also cuts at the home for the elderly on Mondays: people need the days and the way in, and they know her by name. The photograph of the square lies behind the words across the opening, the days follow as plain lines, and the map is a panorama of the lanes around the door for whoever drives in from the next valley. Deep green on warm paper with drawn borders, sturdy as a farm gate. The prices are rows from the top of their page; the two people are rows too, a portrait beside a paragraph; the form stands bare on its page; and the footer is one centred column.

## Salon de la plage

A salon on the front of a seaside resort, open from Easter to the end of September: braids for the children, a blow-dry before a dinner, a trim for whoever forgot to have one before the holidays. It is found on a phone with sand on it, by people who will be gone in ten days, so one screen says it all: the photograph of the promenade behind the name, and the season's week laid across as a strip under it so the only question, whether it is open today, is answered before any other. Teal water and a coral sun, the holiday's own colours. The services are a short list of cards, the work one row of pictures that scrolls, the people a long quiet page, and the appointment one more screen with a bare form; the panorama of the front says which ice-cream kiosk it is beside.

## Quartier étudiant

A salon two streets from the university, where a cut costs what a student can pay and Thursday stays open until nine: its visitors are twenty, book from a phone between two lectures and compare three salons by price alone. Green and orange on a tinted page, bright and current, as a campus is in October. The name stands beside a photograph of somebody's fresh fade, with the facts under it; the services are not rows but a card each on a long page, because there are six of them and each has its student price; the people are rows, a portrait beside what each does best, since a fade and a bob are not cut by the same hands. The pages sit left of the name and the action right of it, and the footer is three lines.

## Coiffeuse à domicile

A hairdresser with no salon at all: she drives to her clients, the housebound, the residents of two care homes on Tuesdays and Thursdays, a bride's family on a Saturday morning. Nobody walks past a door, so there is no window to photograph and the opening is a statement, her name and the towns she covers set large; the days of her rounds follow as plain lines, and the map is a panorama of the area rather than of a street. Cool blue-grey with hairline rules, plain and professional, because it is often a son or a nurse who books for somebody else. The prices are rows from the top of their page; the appointment page sets the invitation beside the form, since the address and the floor matter as much as the day; and the footer is three lines.
