# winery

Fourteen ways one winery is drawn, five by candlelight, five at the cellar door in summer and four on a pale ground. They differ by what brings the visitor up the track, a bottle to buy, a date to come for, a place to see or a story to read, and so by what the first screen leads with: the cellar beside its hours and the way in, one photograph and the name, a board of what is going on, or a long page read before a trip. They differ too in how the wines are read, as bottles to look at, a range led by one cuvée or a price list in rows, and in whether the season's dates are a calendar read in order or a few days that each deserve a look.

## Cave de famille

The estate most people mean: a family, a few hectares and a cellar door open four afternoons a week. The first screen is the cellar by lamplight with this week's hours and the way in beside it, because the visitor is already in the valley and asks whether the door is open and which track to take. The wines and the dates are each a long page that ends on the same ask, come and taste, and the season's dates run down a rule in the order somebody plans in; only the estate's own page is read, a story in a narrow column broken once by its three terraces. Oxblood and gold, with capitals spaced like a wine list.

## Vieille maison

The old house, fourth generation, whose bottles are on restaurant lists and whose customers already know the name. The first screen is one photograph bled to the edges with the name on it and the hours and the gate at its foot, and nothing else asks to be scrolled to. Every other page is read rather than scanned: how the wine is made before the list, with the grand vin first at twice the size of the rest, and why the dates fall where they do before the dates. Black, white and one red with square corners, the name centred over the pages like a label and the footer on one centred column: for a house that sells on its reputation and has no need to raise its voice.

## Route du vin

A destination estate, studied for a week before a weekend away: the visitor comes from Zurich or Lyon, books a tasting and plans the day around it. So home is the long page, the name centred over a wide photograph of the terraces, the week's hours as a strip straight under the opening and a panorama of the tracks, since the way in is half of what is asked. The dates are cards, each a reason to pick that weekend; the wines page tells how they are made before it lists them, and the estate's page is a long look at the place with its pictures in a mosaic. Warm printed paper turned down to lamplight, a burnt-sienna accent and a serif that reads like a travel supplement.

## Caveau du bourg

A cellar in the village street with a tasting bar, open late on Fridays, where neighbours drop in for a glass and leave with three bottles. The words stand beside the photograph of the room on the first screen, with tonight's hours and the door as plain lines next to them. The wines are a list read by name, as on a bar's slate, and the evenings are told first and then set down a rule in a narrow column, since the regulars follow the season from one date to the next. Aubergine with a little antique gold, the name centred like a sign over the door, the estate's pictures one scrolling row as a pause in its page, and a footer of three lines.

## Premier millésime

The newcomers: two growers on rented vines with a first vintage, a borrowed press and no stone cellar worth a photograph yet. No picture is owed, so the opening is the words alone, set large on a violet glow, with the hours and the way in along the foot of the one screen people see when they look the name up on a phone from a wine bar. The few dates, a release and a night in the shed, are cards that each deserve a look, and the wines and the dates are long plain pages; only the estate's page is written out, because how they began is what they have to tell. Violet and magenta under a night sky, a palette that is good for hearth rather than best and right for a cellar with nothing old about it.

## Caveau d’été

The cellar door in summer: tables under the arbour, this year's whites and the rosé in a bucket, people stopping on a bike ride. Home is a short opening over a board of glass panels, the wines, what is on and the estate as tiles with the hours beside the way in, so everything the place has going is seen at once; the dates are a board too, each a card. The wines stay a long page of rows with the price at the right of each. Green and orange on a tinted wash, bright as a July afternoon: for an estate whose season runs from the open cellars in May to the harvest.

## Vente au carton

The estate that sells on price, by the case, to regulars who drive up on a Saturday with the boot empty: they want the price list, the hours and the gate, and no photograph. The opening is a statement set large beside the hours as plain lines and the way in; the wines are rows on a board, read by name and price rather than looked at, and the dates a calendar down a rule on a long page. Deep green on warm paper with borders drawn by hand and hard shadows, plain and sturdy as a wooden crate, and a footer of three lines since the close of every page is the order above it.

## Apéro du vendredi

A young cellar known for what happens after work: Friday on the terrace from five, a food truck in the yard, a concert when the weather holds. It is looked up on a phone at four o'clock to see whether tonight is on, so the dark is pinned, yellow on black like the string of lamps over the tables, and the photograph of the terrace at dusk bleeds to the edges behind the name. The week's hours run as a strip straight under the opening and the way up is a panorama; the wines and the evenings are both boards, every date a card. The pages sit at the left of the header, the name in the middle and the one action at the right.

## Balade des vignes

An estate that is an outing as much as a shop: the walk between the terraces, the picnic under the arbour, the harvest day when children carry the crates. A family decides on a sunny morning, so home is a board under the name centred over a wide photograph, what there is to do, when the cellar is open and how to get there all at once, and the wines are a board too, led by one bottle at twice the size, the one to take home. The year's outings run down a rule on a long page, in the order a family plans them, and the estate's pictures are a mosaic. Peach paper and raspberry in a rounded face, the name centred like a hand-painted sign and the footer on one column.

## Vin nature

A natural-wine estate: nothing added, labels drawn by hand, bottles sold to wine bars and to people who read the back of them. The first screen is one photograph and the name with the hours at its foot, and nothing more to scroll to. The wines page tells how they are made before showing them as tall labels on a pale ground, which is why the light is pinned: the labels are the loud part and the page must stay white behind them. The dates are a quiet calendar down a rule, the estate's page a magazine piece with its pictures in one scrolling row, and the name sits centred over the pages. Black, white and one red.

## Amphore

The estate that sells on craft: long macerations, clay jars buried in the cellar floor, eight hundred bottles of each wine. People read about it before they taste, so home is the long page, the words beside the photograph of the jars, the week's hours as a strip and the lane on a panorama, and the range is led by one cuvée at twice the size, the one the estate is known for. The dates are told as a story in a narrow column, and the estate's page is a long look with its pictures in a mosaic, since the work is worth seeing closely. Terracotta and olive with a chiselled serif, hand-made as the jars.

## Tarif professionnel

The house that sells mostly to restaurants, shops and companies: a buyer wants the list, the prices and the delivery terms, and has seen enough vineyards at sunset. No photograph on the first screen, only the words set large over the hours and the map as plain facts; the wines are rows read by name and price on a long page, and the trade tastings a calendar down a rule. Cool blue-grey with hairline rules and tight corners, a palette that is only fair for a studio and right for this one, because the page has to read like a price sheet. The pages sit at the left of the header with the action at the right, the footer is three lines, and the estate's story is the one page that takes its time.

## Champagne de vigneron

A grower who makes champagne from his own four hectares instead of selling the grapes to a house: a blanc de blancs, a rosé de saignée and one prestige cuvée in a numbered bottle, bought by the case for a wedding or a fortieth. One photograph of the chalk cellar with the name low on it says it, under a header that centres the name over the pages like the label; the hours and the way in are plain lines at its foot. Aubergine and antique gold on a blush page, a little theatrical, as a celebration is. The wines read like a magazine led by the prestige cuvée, drawn twice the size of the others; the few open-cellar days are each a card on a long page; and the estate is a long page with its pictures in one row that scrolls, from the vines in winter to the riddling racks.

## Domaine de la côte

A rosé estate a kilometre from the beach, whose cellar door is busiest at six in the evening in July, when people come up from the sand to taste on the terrace and leave with six bottles in a cool bag. The first screen sets the terrace on one side and today's hours and the way in on the other, the summer week laid across as a strip below. Teal water and a coral sun on a wash of the palette, the coast's own colours. The wines are a long page led by the rosé drawn twice the size; the evenings, a concert, a picnic, the harvest, are cards on a board of glass panels; the estate is a long page of pictures in one row; the map is a panorama of the coast road to the gate; and the footer is three lines.
