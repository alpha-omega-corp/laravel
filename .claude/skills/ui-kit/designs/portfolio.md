# portfolio

Twelve ways one person's work is shown to be hired, seven hung on atelier's white walls and five set in studio's editorial calm. The pages and the prefabs are the same in all of them; what changes is who is looking (a couple, a picture editor, a collector, a producer, a brand manager, a neighbour with a phone), and so what the first screen leads with (one plate, a sentence, a frame bled to the edges, a price), how the work is read (a wall of plates, one story told at length, a strip, an index of ruled rows) and how far down the page the prices stand.

## Accrochage

The portfolio most people picture: one plate and the name on the first screen, then the images hung as numbered plates on one wall and the series on another, so a visitor looks before reading a word. What can be commissioned is told as its author would tell it across a table, one column of prose with the index of prices under it, and the request is the long plain page closed by the title block. For a photographer with a mixed practice of weddings, portraits and reportage, whose clients arrive with no brief beyond having seen one picture somewhere; the one client quoted under the cover is set large, since those are the only borrowed words on the page.

## Tirages signés

For the photographer who sells prints and shows in galleries, studied for a week by a collector or a curator before either writes. Home is the current series told at length under a wide photograph with the title centred over it, like the card at an exhibition's door, and the name stands centred over the pages the same way; the images are a wall where every fifth plate is twice the size, to be looked at closely, and the few series are set across the column, each photograph beside its year and its count. The scheme is pinned light because a print is judged on paper under a lit wall, whatever the visitor's phone prefers, and sandstone's warm stock and serif are a printed catalogue's. The request is a bare form on the page, as a letter would be.

## Grand reportage

A documentary and press photographer read by picture editors, who hire on a beat and a record rather than on one frame: the first screen is a sentence set large with no photograph, under a masthead with the pages at the left, the name in the middle and the action at the right. The images page is the last story told at length with its plates between the paragraphs, the series are tiles on one board to be scanned for a subject, and the day rates are ruled rows under an account of how an assignment runs. Quill's bone paper and blue-black ink are a bound volume's, which is where this work ends up; the editors' words are several and short, so they flow down a wall, and the footer is three plain lines.

## Salle de projection

A filmmaker or a director of photography: the cover is one frame bled to the window's edges, against this look's habit of keeping the plate inside the margins, because a film still is a screen and not a print. Pinned dark, as a screening room is; the stills run as one strip that scrolls through the account of the last film, and the few productions are set across the column, each frame beside who it was for and what it came to. Concrete's signal orange and condensed slab are harsher than a white wall asks for and are chosen anyway: they are a clapperboard's and a flight case's, and the readers are producers. Contact is a second poster, one frame and an address, over a footer of three lines.

## Établi

A product or furniture designer, or the still-life photographer who works for one: the clients are makers and brand managers who want to see the object, then how a commission is run. The first screen sets the words beside the photograph on the long page, the object and its sentence side by side; the images are a board of plates, the series plates three across with the figure as a line of each label, and the offer is told at length, a process in one column over ruled rows of what each part costs. Harbour's cool blue-grey and hairlines read as a specification sheet. Contact is a poster with the form set beside its invitation, for a visitor who has already decided.

## Carnet de croquis

An illustrator: children's books, press drawings, a mural now and then. Home tells the last book at length with plates between the paragraphs; the drawings are a wall where every fifth is twice the size, the books and commissions are tiles on a second board, and what can be ordered is the plain long page. Clay's terracotta and olive on warm paper are louder than this look's white walls want, and suit work that is itself drawn by hand and in colour. The authors' and publishers' words are short and many, so they flow down a wall; contact is one drawing and the address, and the footer one centred column, like a book's last page.

## Caractères

A graphic designer or a typographer, whose work is the type itself: the cover is the name and one sentence set large with no photograph at all, and the first picture comes only on the work page, a long page of plates in the strict grid a designer would draw. The record is long, twenty identities and books rather than six, so the series are an index of ruled rows without photographs, read by client and year; the offer and the request are each one column of prose, the form bare on the page. Vellum's monochrome, square corners and single red are a type specimen's: black letters and one accent, nothing else. For the designer hired by publishers and institutions, who is judged on a list of clients before a single image.

## Long format

The editorial photographer whose work is one story at a time: home is that story set like a magazine piece, a lead photograph, a narrow column and one band of pictures, under a masthead with the name in the middle. The images page reads the same way, the series are set across the column, each photograph beside who it was for and when, and the prices open their own page, ruled as rows read down like a rate card; contact is one portrait and the name. For a photographer hired by magazines, wineries and craftspeople who want to be told about at length, and who choose on tone before price; the one client quoted is set large across the column.

## Jour de noces

A wedding photographer: the first screen is one photograph bled to the window's edges with the name low on it and nothing else to scroll to, under a header with the name centred like an invitation. A couple wants to look at many pictures, so the images are the long page with every fifth twice the size; the weddings are shown whole on the next; and the price list opens its own page, the few formulas as tiles since they are the whole offer. Plum's aubergine and antique gold are quiet and a little ceremonial. The request reads like a letter, the form bare under two paragraphs on how a date is held, and one couple's words carry the home page.

## Scènes de nuit

Concerts, clubs, theatre and festivals: pinned dark, where nocturne's violet turns into a night sky and the panels are edged by a glow rather than a shadow. Home is the long page under a photograph bled to the edges, the words on it; the images run as one strip through an account of a night's work; and the series are what this trade keeps, a long list of dates and venues, so they are an index of ruled rows in the narrow column, with no photographs. Nocturne is electric where this look is calm, which is the point for someone hired by venues and labels. The promoters' words are short and many and flow down a wall; contact is one frame and the name, the footer three lines.

## Studio de quartier

Blossom's soft pink-violet and pills are friendly and unintimidating. Contact is one screen, the form beside its invitation, and the footer three lines under it.

## Tables et maisons

A food and hospitality photographer working for restaurants, hotels and vineyards: the cover is one wide photograph of a table with the words centred over it, like the cover of a menu, in ember's oxblood and gold. The images are told as the story of a service, from the delivery to the last table; the houses worked for are a list in the narrow column, each with its place and year; and the offer is the long page, the kinds of commission as tiles since they are the whole of it. For a photographer whose clients are chefs and hoteliers with no time to read, who want to see a table like theirs and then a form beside its invitation; one chef's words are set large on the cover page.
