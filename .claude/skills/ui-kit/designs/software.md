# software

Fifteen ways one software product is drawn, seven as a launch page, five as a quiet studio and three as a spec sheet of the tool itself. The pages and the prefabs are the same in all of them; what changes is what the first screen leads with (the promise alone, the promise beside the product, or a photograph of it at work), where the proof comes, how the plans are read (side by side, as rows on a board, as a price list in a narrow column) and how much the site argues: the newcomer and the suite, the app decided on a phone in an evening and the platform a committee studies for a week, the house that sells on price and the one that sells restraint.

## Page produit

The front page most products end up with, and for good reason: one promise centred over the product's screen, three things it does, then the names of who already uses it and what they say, and the same ask at the foot. For a product a few years old with clients worth naming, whose visitor arrives from a search and wants to place it in a minute. How to start is a board with the steps down its first tile, the plans stand side by side on a long page, and the demo form sits beside its invitation.

## Lancement

A product in its first year: the promise alone on a dark first screen, set large with nothing beside it, and one early client's words carried across the column underneath, because there are three clients and not three hundred. For the newcomer with one idea and a demo to give, who would rather look sure of one thing than busy with ten. The method is a single row of steps, the plans are a short page with every answer left open, and the footer is three plain lines so the page ends on its ask.

## Prix affichés

A product that wins on its price and says so: the opening is a sentence set large, with no picture to pay for, and the pricing page is a board where the plans lie in rows, each price at the end of its line, beside what every plan includes. For the challenger to a dearer incumbent, whose visitor has the other tab open and compares line by line. Fresh is only fair on launch, louder than a product page usually wants, and is chosen so the cheaper offer looks like the lively one; it is pinned to light because a tariff is read like a printed sheet.

## Grands comptes

A platform sold to groups, where the buyer is a committee that studies it for a week and forwards the page to its IT and its lawyer. The opening is a photograph of the product at work in a firm like theirs, and the proof follows at once: the clients and the certifications ruled as rows with their numbers, then one long quotation from an operations manager. The road to production runs down a timeline because its stages are unequal, the questions stand in two columns, and nothing is a board: every page is read from top to bottom and printed. Denim's indigo and stitched panels say sturdy and made to be used, which is what a group buys.

## Fiche produit

The long product page read from top to bottom like a specification blown up to the size of an exhibition stand: the promise beside the screen, the numbers that prove it as large as the headline, the clients, the voices, and the dark panel that asks again at the foot. For a platform that sells on what it measurably does, two hours saved a week, three tools replaced, whose visitor compares it line by line with the one already paid for. Every page is the long one; the road to production runs down a timeline, the plans stand side by side with the questions in two columns, and the bar is centred.
