# guide

Twelve ways one outfit that takes people outdoors is drawn, seven on the trail and five in the studio. The pages and the prefabs are the same in all of them; what changes is what the first screen leads with (the landscape and the meeting point, the season's dates, one guide's portrait, or the words alone), how the programme is read (dated rows, a card each, or a narrow price list), and whether the site is found on a phone the same morning or studied for a week before a place is asked for.

## Bureau des guides

The valley's guides' office as most people picture it: one landscape edge to edge with the name over its horizon, and the meeting point along the foot of that first screen. The season's outings are dated rows straight under their opening, with the few questions everybody asks already open beneath them, and the booking page sets the invitation beside the form with the map of where the day starts next to its opening. For an office of several guides with a full programme, whose visitors come for a date, a level and a place left.

## École de neige

A ski school that sells weeks to families: the words beside a photograph of the slope, then the long page a parent scrolls on a sofa in October, the weeks as plain dated rows read like a timetable and the long list of parents' questions set in two columns. The pictures get one cover and nothing more, and the booking page holds the meeting point beside its opening, because the question on Monday morning is where to bring the children. Cold blue for a business that exists only while there is snow, and a footer kept to three lines since nearly every page already closes on its band.

## École de grimpe

A climbing school on warm rock in summer: home is told like a route, a wide photograph with the words centred over it, the story of the crag and then where to meet, across the column. The instructors are rows, each with what they teach beside their picture, because pupils choose a person as much as a course; the pictures are one row to swipe, a pause rather than a portfolio. Sandstone instead of the trail's alpine green, for a school whose ground is gneiss and larch and never snow.

## Camp de base

A campsite with a programme: the place itself is what is sold first, so the cover is the meadow and the map sits at its foot, and the week's outings are listed under their opening for whoever is already pitched there. The guides, the pictures and the booking are each a journal page in one narrow column, read on a phone outside a tent, with the form and the map drawn bare, no panel round either. Orchard green, the name centred over the pages like a sign at the gate. For a family site or a hut whose outings are a reason to stay a second week.

## Vol biplace

A paragliding or rafting outfit that sells one thing and is found on a phone at the landing field: the words alone, set large, then the three or four flights as a card each and every answer about weather and weight already open. Pinned light and drawn in the brightest palette the trail takes, only a fair match for the direction but the right one for a business that works on blue-sky days. The pictures are one row to swipe straight under their title, on a long page that closes on the band; the form stands bare beside nothing but the meeting point, and the footer is three lines.

## Peau de phoque

A ski-touring guide who works from December to April and whose clients read the site at night with the avalanche bulletin open beside it: pinned dark, so the snow in the photographs is the only white on the page. Home is the journal of the winter under a cover photograph, the programme a long page whose many questions about fitness and avalanche gear stand in two columns, the guides rows with their ground beside them, and the pictures a cover and a mosaic worth a close look. For a guide whose clients study a route for a week before asking for a place, everything on one axis from the name down to the footer.

## Eaux vives

A kayak or canyoning base by the water: the cover is the river with the words centred over it and the put-in at its foot, since the first question is where to turn up with a towel. A handful of descents, each a card with its level and its price, and a booking page that is the long one, the invitation on one side and the form on the other, closing on the band with the telephone. For a seasonal outfit with a few products and a lot of walk-up trade, in the blue of the water it works on.

## Grandes courses

The guide who sells a few long journeys a year: a full-height photograph with the name low on it, the season told as a magazine piece, and the programme as a price list in one narrow column with nothing beside it, read slowly. The pictures are one cover and a mosaic to look at closely, the guides are portraits, and the request for a place closes a page of prose rather than a sales band. For an established guide whose clients come back every year, and who would rather be read than found.

## Guide privé

One guide who takes one rope at a time: the first screen is his portrait beside his name and nothing else to scroll to, and the page about him is written like a profile, the few people he works with drawn as rows with what each does. A handful of engagements as cards, each worth a look, and a booking page that sets the invitation beside the form, because every request is the start of a conversation about a private day. Warm stone instead of the studio's paper, for a man who sells his own judgement and no programme.

## Haute Route

One great traverse a year, sold like a book: pinned dark, the words alone set large on the first screen, the name centred over the pages. The dates and prices are a narrow list with every answer about huts, fitness and cancellation already open under it, the pictures are a chapter of prose broken once by a band of them, and the form is drawn bare at the end of a page that says how a request becomes a place. A fair palette for the studio, chosen because this journey is remembered by headlamp at four in the morning.

## Marche lente

A hiking leader who sells slow weekends, a walk and a night in a hut, to people who have never worn crampons: soft colours, the words centred over a wide photograph, and almost every page told as a journal. The few weekends are a card each, the questions two reassuring columns, and the pictures a single row that scrolls, a pause in the reading. For an accompagnatrice whose visitors need to be told they can do it before they are told what it costs.

## Bout du monde

Expeditions and far treks, three departures a year that a client studies for weeks: a cover photograph bled to the window's edges over the long home page, and the departures as a narrow list of dates and prices. The leaders are rows with their summits beside them, the pictures a mosaic on a long page, and the request is written at the foot of an account of how a journey is prepared, the form bare. Ember, the colour of a lit tent, for an outfit that sells the evening as much as the summit.
