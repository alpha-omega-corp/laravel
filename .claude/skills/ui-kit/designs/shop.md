# shop

Fifteen ways one shop is drawn, six at the counter, four as a boutique and five as a stall. They differ by what the shop sells and how it is chosen: whether the range is read like a list, admired piece by piece or bought because it came in this week; whether the house is the old sign of its street or the newcomer with a point to make, sold on price or on craft, in town or the last shop for ten kilometres; and whether the page is checked on a phone from the pavement or studied for a week before the visit. The pages and the prefabs are the same in all fifteen; what changes is what the first screen puts in the window.

## Épicerie de quartier

The delicatessen of its street, which is the shop the kit had in mind: regulars who only ever check two things, whether it is open and whether the vacherin has come in. The name beside the counter's photograph on one side, today's hours and the door as plain lines on the other, on the home page and again on the order page, where a basket is phoned through and collected the next morning. The products are rows from the top of their page, the picture beside the words, because the range is read like a shopping list; warm paper and a serif, framed, with nothing on it asking to be admired.

## Bonnes affaires

The shop that sells on price: a discount grocer, a factory outlet, a surplus store, where the visitor wants the figure and the closing time and nothing else. No photograph is owed, so the opening is a statement, the words set large, with the week's hours as the counter's strip straight under it; the products are cards on a rail from the top of their page, the price in the accent. Yellow like a price label with a thick ink edge on every panel; the order page is the long one, with a panorama of the streets around the door for whoever comes with a car boot to fill, and the footer is three lines.

## Vieille enseigne

The old house, on the same corner for a century and in the same family for most of it: an ironmonger, a seed merchant, a grocer whose shopfront is a landmark of its town. The cover is the photograph of that front with the name over it, and the name stands centred above the pages like the painted sign over the door; the long home page then lays the week across as the counter's strip. Deep green with drawn borders, sturdy rather than pretty; the range is long and known, so it is read in rows like a ledger, and the order page holds the hours and an unframed map beside the words, for the customer who phones the order through as their mother did. Everything in the footer sits on one centred column.

## Épicerie de nuit

The corner shop open until midnight, looked up on a phone from the pavement when everything else has closed, which is why the dark is pinned: indigo and the rust of a rivet on black, plain-spoken, made to be used. One board answers the only question (is it open, and where): the name centred over the lit window, then what is on the shelves, tonight's hours and the way in, with no second page to go to. The products are a rail to swipe through with a thumb; the pages sit left of the name and the action right of it, and the footer is three short lines.

## Librairie

A bookshop, or any shop whose goods are words: stationery, records, maps. No photograph says it better than a sentence, so the opening is words alone, set large, in black on white with one red, and the light is pinned because the page should look like paper; the name is centred over the pages like a title page, and today's hours and the door stand beside the opening as plain lines with no panel. The range is led by one thing, the book of the month drawn twice the size of the others, on a long page with the bookseller's reasons under it; ordering is a long page too, since a title that is not on the shelf is ordered and collected. Vellum is only fair at a counter and right here.

## Boutique de créateurs

A boutique of things made by hand (ceramics, clothes, jewellery), the newcomer of a street people stroll along rather than shop in. The work is the loud part: one photograph across the whole opening with the name low on it, then the pieces as portrait tiles with air around them, their prices in the display face, from the top of their page. Black, white and one red with square corners; everything else is as the studio draws it, the hours a quiet centred list and the map in grey, because nothing on the page should compete with the objects.

## Savonnerie

A shop sold on craft and studied for a week before the visit: a soap maker, a perfumer, a chocolatier, where one thing in the window carries the season. The name is centred over a wide photograph and again over the pages, like the label on a tin; the range opens on its leading item drawn twice the size, and the rest follow as tiles. Soft pink and violet with pills and no borders, good here rather than best and right for a shop that wraps everything in tissue. People travel to it, so the week is laid across as a strip and the map is a panorama of the streets around the door.

## Magasin du village

The village shop an hour from town, run by a cooperative or by the farmers themselves and the last one for ten kilometres: people drive out for it and want to know that it is worth the road, which days, and which road. The long home page opens on the shop's photograph behind the words, then the bento of what the valley makes, then the hours beside the map. Sunny yellow with thick ink edges, the colour of the sign on the cantonal road; the range is led by the village's own cheese, drawn twice the size of the rest, the order page holds the words beside the hours and the door, and everything in the footer sits on one centred column.

## Confiserie

A sweet shop, a toy shop, an ice-cream counter: the shop children pull their parents into on a Saturday, on foot. Peach paper and raspberry in a rounded face, good for a stall and exactly right for this one; the name is centred over a wide photograph of the jars and again over the pages, like the lettering on the shop window. Under it one board holds the treats of the week, the week's hours laid across as a strip and a panorama of the streets around the door; the sweets are rows read by name and price, and the order page is the long one, for birthday bags ordered ahead.

## Céramiste

A potter who throws in the back room and sells in the front: bowls, jugs and plates in a few glazes, each a little different, bought by people who stop in the street because of the window and come back with a list of wedding presents. The name stands beside one photograph of a shelf of bowls, the range is a long page led by the piece of the season drawn twice the size of the rest, and the story of the kiln and the clay reads down its own long page. Terracotta and olive on warm paper, a serif cut with a chisel, the colours of the glazes themselves. The open days are a strip across the page, since the shop shuts when the kiln is firing; the order page is long and quiet; and the footer is one centred column.

## Marée du jour

A fish shop on the harbour, whose stock is whatever the two boats brought in at six: sole on Tuesday, mackerel all summer, oysters from September. The visitor checks it before deciding what is for dinner, so the first screen sets the morning's catch beside the hours and the way in, and the week's opening runs across as a strip, because the shop shuts on the days the boats stay in. Teal water and a coral sun on a wash of the palette, the colours of the quay. The catch is rows with the picture beside the name and the price per kilo, read more than looked at; the order page sets the form beside the hours and the map, for the platter collected on Sunday morning.

## Quincaillerie

The hardware shop that has everything: twenty thousand references in drawers, keys cut while you wait, paint mixed to the sample and a man behind the counter who knows the screw by its sound. It is looked up to know whether it is open on Saturday afternoon and whether it has the part, so the home is a board, the shop's departments, the hours and the way in visible at once. Cool blue-grey with hairline rules, dense and professional, like the catalogue on the counter. The range is rows from the top of their page with the picture beside each line; the order page sets the form beside the hours and the map, for whoever orders a part and collects it; and the footer is three lines.
