# legal

Twelve ways one legal office is drawn, seven on ledger's paper and five in studio's air. The pages and the prefabs are the same in all of them; what changes is what a visitor is handed first — the registers the firm is entered in, the price of the first meeting, the door and the hours, or the face of the person who would take the file — and how much of the firm is told as a letter rather than laid out as a list.

## Étude de la vieille ville

The established firm of a cantonal capital, chosen on standing: two partners, a notary, a brass plate that has been on the same door for a generation. The first screen is one sentence set large with no photograph and the name centred over the pages like a letterhead, and the registers are ruled as a list on that same page; the fields are read down as rows with the office held beside them, the people are told in one column, and the first meeting is asked for beside the map. For the visitor who compares two or three firms over a week and wants to see who is admitted where before anybody's face.

## Notaire de bourg

A country notary and one associate in a market town, where the office has drawn the same families' deeds for three generations. Home is a letter in one column, the deeds and what each costs are the list their page is for, each person is a row with what they do beside them, every answer stands open because the people who read it do not click to find out, and the way in sits beside the form with nothing drawn round the map. Vellum's black on white with one red is the deed and its seal.

## Permanence de quartier

A walk-in legal counter that sells on a fixed price and a short wait: the first screen is the opening in two halves, the price of a first meeting and the one thing to do beside the door, under the line that says when the phone is answered, because most visitors are on a phone with a letter in the other hand and a deadline printed on it. The fields are the price list, read down from the top of their page; the answers all stand open; the form is bare on the page and the streets round the door are drawn across it. Sandstone's warm paper, because a counter people walk into should not look like chambers.

## Fiduciaire

A fiduciary and tax adviser whose clients arrive in March with a folder of papers and want three things: the hours, the deadline and the fee per return. The opening is two halves under the line that says when the phone is answered, the services a statement of account with a figure at the end of every line, the bodies it belongs to one muted row of marks rather than a register, and the request for a meeting an invitation on one side and the form on the other. Harbour, cool and dense, for a firm whose work is figures.

## Groupe romand

A group with offices in several towns and thirty lawyers, read by in-house counsel comparing firms for a mandate. A photograph of the building lies behind the opening, the pages sit at the left of the bar and the action at its right, the fields are tiles to scan rather than a list to read, the memberships a quiet row of marks, the people one long page, and the office that takes the first meeting stands beside its form. Pinned light: its pages are printed into tender files, and quill's bone paper is the group's letterhead.

## Avocat de la défense

A litigator's chambers, one or two names, rung at night by somebody whose brother has just been arrested. No photograph anywhere: the opening is the sentence alone, large, on a dark page in oxblood and gold, and home reads as one column down to the registers. The answers stand open because nobody in that hour opens an accordion, and the request is a bare form at the end of a short letter. Ember is only fair on ledger and is chosen for that: it is the one palette here that does not look like an office that has closed for the day.

## Médiation

A mediator and family lawyer who works by agreement: both parties read the site, and neither should feel the other's lawyer wrote it. Every page but the list is told in one measured column, as a letter would be — the firm, the people, the questions and the request for a first meeting — with each person a row, the registers a ruled list, and nothing drawn round the map. Sandstone's warm paper, the name centred over the pages and the footer one centred column: nothing on the page takes a side.

## Jeune barreau

Three lawyers in their thirties who opened last year and have no brass plate yet: what they have is themselves. The cover is one photograph of them filling the first screen, the name centred over the pages, the fields are tiles with an icon over each, the people are set like a magazine article, and the questions run in two columns on a wide page. Vellum's black, white and one red makes a new firm look decided.

## De mère en fille

A family firm, the founder and her daughter at the same table, with a story its clients tell each other. Home, the people and the questions are told as articles, the fields open their page as studio draws them, small cards under a centred heading, and each of the two is a row with a portrait beside what she does. The page that asks for the meeting is the one long page: the invitation beside the form, the street drawn across under it. Ember's oxblood and gold on warm paper is the old house in a young layout.

## Avocate seule

A sole practitioner, mostly family and employment matters, whose clients choose her and not a firm. The first screen is her portrait beside her name and the one thing to do, the fields are a short ruled list with a fee at the end of each line, she and her assistant are two rows, every answer stands open and the form is bare on the page. Blossom, soft and without a border anywhere, for clients who arrive worried and feared a lawyer's office would look like one.

## Droit du numérique

A boutique for start-ups, software and data: founders find it at midnight between two releases and want to read what it does before they see who does it. A sentence alone on a night sky, the pages at the left of the bar and the action at its right, the offers in ruled rows with a price at the end of each, the questions in two columns and the bar's and the chambers' marks in one muted row. Pinned dark: nocturne is only fair in studio by day, and the night is what this firm looks like.

## Bord du lac

A private-client firm in a lakeside town: successions, property and families who moved here from abroad and study a firm for a week before writing. The photograph lies behind the words, home and the questions read as a magazine, the fields are tiles with a large icon over each, the people a long page of portraits, and the request closes a short article with the map plain under it. Lagoon's teal and coral on a pale aqua page is the water outside its window.
