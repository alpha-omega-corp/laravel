# bakery

Fifteen ways one bakery is drawn: six at the counter, five on the market's board and four in the pâtissier's studio. The pages and the prefabs are the same in all of them; what changes is who is looking (the regular at six, the office at noon, the parents at half past three, the couple ordering a wedding cake) and so what the first screen leads with, the hours, the day's batch or one cake, and how the range is read: as rows with their prices, on a board, or as a display case.

## Comptoir du matin

The neighbourhood bakery most people look up before leaving the house: the name beside this week's hours and the door on the first screen, and the breads in rows with their prices from the top of their page. For an old counter whose regulars ask two things, whether it is open yet and what a loaf costs.

## Four du village

The only shop in a village, drawn like the notice board by its door: the bread, the cakes, the hours and the way in as tiles of one board, under a name that stands centred over the pages like a shop sign. For a country bakery people drive to from the hamlets around, most of all on Sunday morning; its range page keeps the hours pinned beside the list, which is led by the loaf the house is known for, and the footer closes on one centred column as a signboard would.

## Pain quotidien

The bakery that sells on price and on being open: no photograph in the opening at all, the words set large, when the bread comes out, with the week's hours and the map as plain lines beside them, so on a phone the hours are the second thing on the screen. For a bakery by a station or a school gate, found on a phone from the pavement; its range is one long page of rows, a name and a price, read with a thumb. Denim is plain-spoken where sandstone is printed: a workday counter, not a heritage one.

## Fournil de nuit

The craft bakery that sells the fire: the mouth of a wood oven behind the name, oxblood and gold in the dark, the name in the middle of the bar like a sign over a door. For a house whose bread is baked at night in a wood-fired oven and costs what that costs; the loaves keep the counter's own rail of square photographs, each one worth looking at, with the hours held beside them. Ember is only fair at a counter, which is a bright place, and pinned dark it is the right one for the one counter that works at night.

## Levain

The range is a board of glass panels led by the loaf of the week, and the story of the oven is the long page. Sandstone's printed paper and burnt sienna are a fire on a market morning, warmer than the stall's usual daylight.

## Chocolatier

A pâtissier-chocolatier whose year turns on four weeks before Christmas and two before Easter, when boxes are ordered for families and companies: one photograph of a box opened bled behind the name, the name centred over the pages like the gold on its lid, and the range a long page led by the season's box at twice the size, each praline and each cake with its price. For a house people visit to give rather than to eat, who want the box, the price and the last day to order. Ember's oxblood and gold on warm paper are the ribbon and the foil, and its spaced capitals the label.
