# site-footer

The foot of every public page: the name and a line about the place, how to reach it, the links and the small print, and the door to the admin, which it draws itself whenever the site has one.

## Placement

- marketing.footer — first: under the band, in three columns from md for the name, the contact and the links, one under another on a phone
- split.footer — first: the page's close, since split has no band
- carte.footer — first: under the band
- poster.footer — first: the close under a quiet main, since poster has no band
- board.footer — first: under the board, and its close unless the last tile is a cta-band
- journal.footer — first: the colophon, which the page closes on instead of a band
- * — never: a site page has one foot, the footer of a site layout, and an app screen or the admin draws none of the site's

## Limits

- links — 9; advice 7 (millers-law, hicks-law)
- per-page — 1 (jakobs-law)

## Behaviour

- Every page draws the same footer, with the same name, contact and links in the same order, so the contact is where a visitor looks for it on any page. (jakobs-law, law-of-similarity)
- It carries the address, the phone and the email whenever the brief gives them: the phone dialled by its digits, the email a mailto link, and the address no more precise than the brief, since a street number nobody gave is a door somebody walks to. (jakobs-law, fittss-law, cognitive-bias)
- Where the layout has no band, as in split, poster, journal and a board without a closing tile, it is the page's close, and one with no contact ends the page on nothing a visitor can act on. (peak-end-rule)
- Its links are what a visitor looks for at a page's foot, such as the pages, the legal notice and the privacy policy, and never the admin's door, which the kit draws itself, small and secondary. (jakobs-law, von-restorff-effect)
- The blurb is a line about the place and the note the small print, each only as the brief states it: a founding year or a label nobody gave is a false claim. (cognitive-bias)
- The pages and the contact each stand under a small heading in the accent, after the name and its line, and the pages default to the site's own, so the foot of a long page has the way on. (law-of-common-region, jakobs-law)
- Its arrangement is the site's, chosen on deployer's mockup and written by the build as `variant`, the same on every page: `centred`, everything on one centred column, or `compact`, the name, the pages and the contact each on a line with no headings, for a page whose close is above it. With none, the direction draws it its own way. (law-of-similarity, peak-end-rule)
- Who made the site is one small link in the bottom bar, `credit` with their name, link and mark, after the small print and before the admin's door, and never above the business's own name or contact. (law-of-proximity, von-restorff-effect)
