# projects

Work done, as the proof it is: a card per project with its photograph, where and when it was built, what it was and the one figure that sizes it, for a builder, an architect, an engineer or a software house.

## Placement

- carte.offer — fits: the work the page is for, in the widest column, as many cards across as fit
- carte.main — fits: under the offer, the jobs that prove it
- marketing.main — fits: across the reading width, three cards across at most and one on a phone
- split.main — fits: under the opening's two halves
- poster.main — fits: the three that matter, below the fold, since the cover has already said what the firm builds
- board.main — fits: a tile of its own, one card across in a third of the page and two in the first 2×2 tile
- journal.main — fits: the work in the narrow column, one card across, each read as a short story
- stacked.main — fits: one card of the grid, a third of the width
- focus.main — fits: the project the one task is about, in the 36rem column
- console.main — fits: the admin's view of what a visitor sees, in its column of data
- workspace.detail — fits: one block of the chosen item
- * — never: nothing wide goes in a side column, an opening says what the page is and a band asks for one thing

## Limits

- title — 48 characters (law-of-pragnanz)
- projects — 12; advice 6 (choice-overload, pareto-principle)
- per-page — 1 (law-of-similarity)

## Behaviour

- It lives on the one page whose role is the work, and home shows the three that matter most and links to the rest rather than listing every job twice. (pareto-principle, choice-overload)
- A bare tag draws the projects the owner keeps in the admin, and one written on the tag is the brief's own: never an invented client, place, surface or year, since a figure on a builder's page is a claim somebody can check. (cognitive-bias, teslers-law)
- Each card carries one figure and what it counts, the number a visitor will remember, and the second and the third wait on the project's own page. (von-restorff-effect, working-memory)
- The place and the year lead each card in one line, so a row is scanned by where and when before any of it is read. (serial-position-effect, chunking)
- A project with no photograph keeps the photograph's frame, and the frame is never filled with a picture of somebody else's building. (law-of-similarity, cognitive-bias)
- A card that opens the project's own page is one link over the whole card, named by the project and never by “voir le projet”. (fittss-law, paradox-of-the-active-user)
- Linked from the navigation as #realisations, it carries id="realisations" on its own tag, or the link lands nowhere. (flow, peak-end-rule)
- Its arrangement is chosen on its tag and the direction's look stays: `variant="rows"` sets each project across the column, the photograph beside its facts, for a few large jobs; `variant="index"` drops the photographs and lists the record as ruled rows, for a long one; bare, it is the direction's own. (law-of-common-region, aesthetic-usability-effect)
