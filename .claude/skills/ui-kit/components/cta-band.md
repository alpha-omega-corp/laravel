# cta-band

The page's close: the last thing a visitor is asked to do, to book, order, call or come by, with the facts that make it possible, on a ground of its own before the footer.

## Placement

- marketing.band — first: across the full width before the footer, on the band's own ground, the accent in hearth and the ink in counter
- carte.band — first: the same close after the list and whatever main holds, for booking or ordering
- board.main — fits: the board's last tile, since board has no band, its copy, actions and details wrapping to the tile's width
- * — never: a page has one close, the band or a board's last tile, and split, poster and journal close on their footer instead

## Limits

- links — 2 (hicks-law)
- title — 48 characters (law-of-pragnanz)
- lead — 160 characters (cognitive-load)
- per-page — 1 (peak-end-rule)

## Behaviour

- It is the last thing on the page before the footer, and it asks for the one thing the page is for: in a board it is the last tile and never the first. (peak-end-rule, serial-position-effect)
- Its first link is the page's primary action, most often where the header's action goes, so the page asks for one thing at both ends; a second link is a secondary button. (von-restorff-effect, serial-position-effect)
- Its link labels are verbs of the task in the site's language, such as Réserver or Commander, never “en savoir plus” or “click here”. (paradox-of-the-active-user)
- Its details are the facts that make the action possible, the address, the hours and the phone, only as the brief gives them, and each is a link where it can be one: tel:, mailto: or the map's search. (cognitive-bias, fittss-law)
- Its phone and email are the site's one phone and one email, the same ones the header and the footer give. (teslers-law)
