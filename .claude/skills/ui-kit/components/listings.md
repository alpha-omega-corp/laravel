# listings

Things offered one at a time, each with the facts it is chosen by, its price and where it stands: a flat with its rooms and surface, a car with its year and mileage.

## Placement

- carte.offer — fits: the list the page is for, in the widest column, as many cards across as fit
- carte.main — fits: under the offer, what else is available
- marketing.main — fits: across the reading width, three cards across at most and one on a phone
- split.main — fits: under the opening's two halves
- poster.main — fits: the newest few, below the fold, since a long list is carte's
- board.main — fits: a tile of its own, one card across in a third of the page and two in the first 2×2 tile
- journal.main — fits: what is on offer, one card across in the narrow column
- stacked.main — fits: one card of the grid, a third of the width
- focus.main — fits: the one object the task is about, in the 36rem column
- console.main — fits: the admin's view of what a visitor sees, in its column of data
- workspace.detail — fits: one block of the chosen item
- * — never: nothing wide goes in a side column, an opening says what the page is and a band asks for one thing

## Limits

- title — 48 characters (law-of-pragnanz)
- listings — 12; advice 9 (choice-overload, hicks-law)
- per-page — 1 (law-of-similarity)

## Behaviour

- It lives on the one page whose role is what is on offer, and home shows the newest three and links to the rest rather than the whole stock twice. (choice-overload, pareto-principle)
- A bare tag draws the listings the owner keeps in the admin, and one written on the tag is the brief's own: never an invented flat, a price or a mileage, since each is an offer somebody may act on. (cognitive-bias, teslers-law)
- Every listing gives the same three to five facts in the same order, rooms, surface, floor, so two are compared by reading down one column. (law-of-similarity, working-memory)
- What has gone says so in the owner's word, Réservé or Vendu, and stays a short while as proof of what the business sells, rather than vanishing or looking available. (mental-model, cognitive-bias)
- The price is printed as written, CHF 1 850.–/mois or Prix sur demande, and is never left to look like zero. (postels-law, cognitive-bias)
- A listing with no photograph keeps the photograph's frame, and the frame is never filled with a picture of another object. (law-of-similarity, cognitive-bias)
- It is not the catalogue: a catalogue sells the same thing again tomorrow and can be given a checkout, a listing is one object that ends and is asked about. (mental-model, jakobs-law)
- Its arrangement is chosen on its tag and the direction's look stays: `variant="rows"` sets each listing across the column, the photograph beside its facts, for a short stock read closely; `variant="feature"` draws the first one large, for a stock led by one object; bare, it is the direction's own. (von-restorff-effect, aesthetic-usability-effect)
