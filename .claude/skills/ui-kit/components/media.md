# media

The one element that holds a picture on its own, the owner's photograph or one from the library, and until there is one, a frame captioned with what belongs in it.

## Placement

- marketing.main — fits: across the reading width at its own ratio, never taller than about three quarters of a screen
- split.main — fits: under the opening's two halves
- carte.main — fits: under the list and its hours
- carte.* — never: a photograph belongs in main, never between the lead and the list or in the column held beside it
- board.main — fits: a tile of its own, the first 2×2 one when the picture is what the board is about; two or three tiles may carry one and the rest are type
- journal.gallery — fits: the one band of pictures across the page, breaking the column once
- journal.* — never: the column is words, broken once, by the gallery
- poster.main — never: poster spends its boldness on the cover, and its main stays plain
- * — never: an opening draws its picture through the hero, a side column holds the hours and the way in and a band is the page's ask, and an app screen is not a site's

## Behaviour

- It always has a subject in the page's language: with a picture it is the alt text, and without one the caption naming the photograph owed, so a frame is never empty or unexplained. (aesthetic-usability-effect, postels-law)
- A photograph of the business is the owner's upload and nothing else, never a stock picture of somebody else's room or a drawing passed off as one; until it arrives the frame keeps its subject and no src. (cognitive-bias)
- A picture that is not the owner's photograph comes from deployer's library, taken in the page's own look, and never a drawing in colours the kit does not define. (law-of-similarity, aesthetic-usability-effect)
- Only the opening's picture is fetched at once: a media placed below the opening never takes priority, and loads lazily. (doherty-threshold)
