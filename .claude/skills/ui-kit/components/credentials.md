# credentials

What vouches for the business, glanced at rather than read: the certifications it holds, the bodies it belongs to and the clients it has worked for, each a name, a detail and its mark.

## Placement

- marketing.main — fits: a quiet row across the reading width, under the proof it backs
- split.main — fits: under the opening's two halves
- carte.main — fits: under the offer, what stands behind it
- poster.main — fits: one row below the fold
- board.main — fits: a tile of its own, the marks two across in a third of the page
- journal.story — fits: the standing of whoever tells the story, in the narrow column
- journal.main — fits: the practical part of the narrow column, two across
- stacked.main — fits: one card of the grid, a third of the width
- focus.main — fits: what reassures before the one task, in the 36rem column
- split.visit — fits: a short list of three or four under the hours, since a credential is a fact read in passing
- carte.visit — fits: three or four in the 22rem column beside the list, what stands behind what is listed
- poster.facts — fits: one row at the foot of the cover, where a business with no hours to give says who vouches for it
- * — never: an opening is the business in its own words, a band asks for one thing, and a row of marks in either is decoration

## Limits

- title — 48 characters (law-of-pragnanz)
- credentials — 12; advice 6 (selective-attention, choice-overload)
- per-page — 1 (occams-razor)

## Behaviour

- A session building a page never writes one from memory: a bare tag draws what the owner entered, and a certification, a licence number or a client named on a page without the brief saying so is a false statement the firm answers for. (cognitive-bias, teslers-law)
- Each one is named as its issuer writes it, with the detail that makes it checkable, the number, the year, the scope, and links to the register that confirms it when there is one. (mental-model, postels-law)
- It is quiet by design, smaller than the sections around it and never the page's loud part, because it is glanced at for reassurance and not read. (selective-attention, aesthetic-usability-effect)
- A mark is the issuer's own file drawn at one height, and with none the name is set in type: never a redrawn logo or a badge made up to look official. (law-of-similarity, cognitive-bias)
- Six that matter beat twelve that include every membership: the ones a client in this trade looks for come first. (pareto-principle, serial-position-effect)
- It stands beside the claim it backs, under the work or above the ask, and not alone at the foot where nobody connects it to anything. (law-of-proximity, goal-gradient-effect)
- Its arrangement is chosen on its tag and the direction's look stays: `variant="logos"` draws only the marks in one muted row, for clients known by their logo; `variant="list"` rules them as rows, the name beside its detail, for licences read by their number; bare, it is the direction's own. (law-of-similarity, aesthetic-usability-effect)
