# map

Where the business is: a map of its address with the address under it and a link that opens directions, and nothing at all when there is no address.

## Placement

- split.visit — fits: under the hours in the opening's other half, 4/3 on a phone and 16/9 from sm, never taller than 30rem
- carte.visit — fits: under the hours in the 22rem column, held in view from lg
- poster.facts — fits: beside the hours at the foot of the cover from md
- marketing.main — fits: in the reading width, beside the hours in market
- split.main — fits: with the hours, under the opening
- carte.main — fits: with the hours, under the list
- poster.main — fits: with the hours, below the fold
- board.main — fits: a tile of its own, beside the hours
- journal.main — fits: after the hours, in the narrow column
- stacked.main — fits: one card of the grid
- focus.main — fits: beside the one task it serves, a pick-up's address
- console.main — fits: the admin's view of what a visitor sees, in its column of data
- workspace.detail — fits: one block of the chosen item
- * — never: an opening says what the page is, the band's details carry the address as a line and the footer's contact is its own

## Limits

- title — 48 characters (law-of-pragnanz)
- per-page — 1 (occams-razor)

## Behaviour

- It sits in the same region as the hours, so when and where are read as one. (law-of-common-region, law-of-proximity)
- Its address is the brief's and no more precise: a street number nobody gave pins a door somebody walks to. (cognitive-bias)
- Where the address misleads, as a farm down a road with no number does, its title says how to find the way in, since the pin only brings a visitor near. (mental-model)
- A bare tag draws the site's own address, the one the footer and the band give, so the site names one place. (teslers-law, law-of-similarity)
- Linked from the navigation as #acces, it carries id="acces" on its own tag, or the link lands nowhere. (flow, peak-end-rule)
