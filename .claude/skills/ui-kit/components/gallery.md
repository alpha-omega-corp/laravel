# gallery

Pictures the business chooses, in its order, each with a caption when it has one: a salon's portfolio, a restaurant's room, a florist's weddings, a carpenter's work.

## Placement

- carte.offer — fits: the work the page is for, in the widest column, as many pictures across as fit
- carte.main — fits: under the offer, the pictures of what the list sells
- marketing.main — fits: across the reading width, as many pictures across as fit and one on a phone
- split.main — fits: under the opening's two halves
- poster.main — fits: a few, below the fold, since the poster's own photograph is the opening
- board.main — fits: a tile of its own, one picture across in a third of the page and two or three in the first 2×2 tile
- journal.main — fits: the work in the narrow column, two pictures across
- stacked.main — fits: one card of the grid, a third of the width
- focus.main — fits: the pictures the one task is about, in the 36rem column
- * — never: nothing wide goes in a side column, an opening already holds its own photograph and a band asks for one thing

## Limits

- title — 48 characters (law-of-pragnanz)
- pictures — 24; advice 12 (choice-overload, working-memory)
- per-page — 1 (choice-overload)

## Behaviour

- It lives on the one page whose role is the gallery or the work, and home links to that page rather than showing the pictures again. (choice-overload, hicks-law)
- A bare tag draws the pictures the owner chooses in the admin, and a picture written on the tag is the business's own: never a stock photograph passed off as its work. (teslers-law, cognitive-bias)
- Every picture is the same kind of thing at the same size, so the eye reads the grid as one set and the odd one out is the one the owner meant. (law-of-similarity, law-of-common-region)
- A caption says what the picture shows in the business's words, a word or a line, and a picture whose alt and caption are both empty is decoration. (cognitive-load, postels-law)
- Linked from the navigation as #galerie, it carries id="galerie" on its own tag, or the link lands nowhere. (flow, peak-end-rule)
