# deals

The house's offers: what costs less, when it holds and how to take it, an offer a card with its day, its price beside the usual one and one way in, never a checkout.

## Placement

- carte.offer — never: the offer column is the list the page is for, and an offer is a few things beside it
- carte.main — fits: under the offer, what of the list costs less and when
- marketing.main — fits: across the reading width, two to four offers side by side and one under another on a phone
- split.main — fits: under the opening's two halves
- poster.main — fits: below the fold, the offers in one row
- board.main — fits: a tile of its own, the offers one under another
- journal.main — fits: the practical part of the narrow column, the offers one under another
- stacked.main — fits: one card of the grid, the offers one under another
- focus.main — fits: beside the one task, in the 36rem column
- * — never: offers are compared across a width a side column does not have, an opening says what the page is, and a band asks for one thing rather than offering four

## Limits

- title — 48 characters (law-of-pragnanz)
- intro — 160 characters (cognitive-load)
- deals — 4; advice 3 (hicks-law, choice-overload)
- note — 160 characters (cognitive-load)
- per-page — 1 (occams-razor)

## Behaviour

- It stands on the page of the offer, beside or under the menu it discounts, and home links to it rather than repeating it, because an offer read twice reads as a sale that never ends. (choice-overload, cognitive-bias)
- Every offer says when it holds, a day, an evening, a date it ends, since an offer with no day is a price and belongs on the menu. (mental-model, cognitive-bias)
- A price is printed as the owner wrote it and the usual one is struck through beside it only when the owner gives it: never a percentage worked out for them, never a crossed-out price nobody charged. (cognitive-bias, teslers-law)
- At most one offer carries a flag, in the owner's word, so the one that is new or ends tonight stands out from the rest. (von-restorff-effect, selective-attention)
- An offer's button leads somewhere, the menu, the basket or the phone, under a label that says where, and never takes a payment itself. (mental-model, jakobs-law)
- What the offers exclude stands under them as one line, the delivery, the drinks, one per order, rather than in a footnote. (cognitive-bias, peak-end-rule)
- Linked from the navigation as #offres, it carries id="offres" on its own tag, or the link lands nowhere. (flow, peak-end-rule)
- Its arrangement is chosen on its tag and the direction's look stays: `variant="ticket"` cuts each offer as a ticket, notched at its sides and torn along a dashed line above its price, for a voucher handed over the counter; `variant="banner"` sets each offer across the column on a strip of the highlight, its price large at the end, for one or two offers worth the whole width; bare, it is the direction's own. (aesthetic-usability-effect, von-restorff-effect)
