# split

The opening is two halves — what the business is on one side, when it is open and how to get there
on the other — then its sections, and the footer as the close. The first screen answers the two
questions a walk-in customer has.

## Shell

`navigation/navbars/*`

## Suits

a shop, a bakery, a butcher, a farm shop — anywhere people walk into

## Not for

A business with nothing to visit — `marketing`. One whose list is why people come — `carte`.

## Regions

What a roll places in each region of this layout's stub, and what each region is.

- nav: the bar across the top — the name, the page's anchors and the one thing to do
- nav — site-header
- hero: the opening's first half — the name, what it is and the one thing to do, or the photograph that says it
- hero — hero
- visit: the opening's other half — when it is open and how to get there, beside the hero on a wide screen and under it on a phone; the hero takes the whole width when this is empty
- visit — schedule, map | nothing
- main: the sections under the opening, one under another
- main — prefabs, features | section | nothing, media | nothing
- footer: contact and links, which is the page's close — split has no band
- footer — site-footer

## Collapse

The two halves stack, the hero first; the visit half follows it straight away, still in the first
two screenfuls.

## Beyond the recipe

Keep the visit half short — the hours and the map, or the hours and a line of address. Anything
longer belongs in main, or the opening stops being one screen.
